## Architecture convergence

The three modules share one Action lifecycle, one Evidence system, one organisation/location security model, one Client directory, one Workforce directory, shared management surfaces and append-only assurance histories. No `IncidentAction`, `ComplaintAction` or `SafeguardingAction` parallel lifecycle was introduced.

Domain meaning remains separate:

- Incident retains event, harm and causal-investigation semantics.
- Complaint retains acknowledgement, communication, response and remedy semantics.
- Safeguarding retains immediate safety, referral and external safeguarding semantics.

The assurance distinctions remain enforced: creation is not investigation; Action completion is not verification; verification is not effectiveness; effectiveness is not management assurance; management assurance does not automatically reduce Risk; response/referral activity does not automatically close a Complaint or Safeguarding concern.

## Closure assurance

- Unresolved canonical Actions and unmet evidence/effectiveness requirements block closure where required.
- Complaint response, external Safeguarding referral and Incident investigation activity do not independently satisfy closure.
- Completing an Action does not automatically verify completion, establish effectiveness, provide management assurance or alter Risk.
- A convergence defect was corrected so an Incident governance editor who lacks the applicable closure authority cannot reopen a High Incident through the direct assurance API.

## Closed-record protection

- Material generic edits to closed Incident, Complaint and Safeguarding records are rejected server-side.
- Formal reopening is required before governed work resumes.
- Stale/generic status manipulation cannot silently rewrite a closed record.

## Reopening and history

- Assurance, closure and reopening decisions are append-only.
- Earlier decisions remain visible with actor, authority, time, rationale and evidence context.
- Reopening creates a new historical decision and does not overwrite the prior closure.

## RM administrative-burden review

- Known organisation, location, Client, Staff, source, Action and Evidence records are selected or linked rather than re-entered.
- Action handoff pre-populates safe context while leaving the professional decision editable.
- Complaint and Safeguarding data are progressively disclosed instead of being required in one oversized initial form.
- Safeguarding has a dedicated RM-burden regression proving concise initial capture without duplicate source narratives.
- Closure-readiness views state what is missing instead of requiring the RM to search across modules.
- My Work uses actionable exception wording such as investigation, response, referral, evidence, effectiveness or authorised-closure requirements.
- Calendar entries are limited to explicit, meaningful provider-set dates; the product does not invent statutory timescales.
- Structured records drive branded reports rather than requiring management to rewrite the chronology.

## Known limitations

- External referrals and notifications are recorded and governed; they are not transmitted automatically to third parties.
- QCGMS does not autonomously make safeguarding, closure, effectiveness or other professional decisions.
- Closure-authority role sets are currently code-governed rather than tenant-configurable policy matrices.
- A broad typed cross-module Governance Relationship architecture is not introduced in this release.
- The combined dashboard provides a dense exception surface and should receive observed-user usability testing after release; it was not redesigned during convergence.
- Incident continues to use existing governed register/reporting surfaces rather than adding another dedicated per-record report workflow in this release.
- Local query timing is not a substitute for production observability and concurrency testing.
- One Windows loopback navigation suspension passed on retry and should be monitored if it recurs in CI.
